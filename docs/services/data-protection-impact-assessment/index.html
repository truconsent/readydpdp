Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

A structured, evidence-based assessment of privacy risk for high-risk processing activities, required for Significant Data Fiduciaries and good practice for everyone else.

What it is

A DPIA is a structured evaluation of the necessity, proportionality and risk of a specific processing activity — typically triggered by new products, sensitive processing, large-scale profiling, or a change in purpose. Significant Data Fiduciaries are required to conduct DPIAs periodically; we recommend the discipline to any organisation launching a materially new use of personal data.

Who needs this

Significant Data Fiduciaries with a periodic DPIA obligation, and any organisation launching a new product, AI feature, or processing activity that materially changes what personal data is collected or how it is used.

Our process

  1. Trigger assessment

    We confirm whether the activity in question genuinely warrants a DPIA versus a lighter-weight privacy check.

  2. Data flow mapping

    We map the specific data flows, systems and third parties involved in the activity under assessment.

  3. Necessity & proportionality test

    We evaluate whether the data collected is minimised to what is genuinely necessary for the stated purpose.

  4. Risk scoring

    We score identified risks by likelihood and severity to Data Principals, not just to the business.

  5. Mitigation plan & sign-off

    We produce a mitigation plan and a formal DPIA report suitable for board or regulator review.

Frequently asked questions

Who is legally required to conduct DPIAs?

Significant Data Fiduciaries are required under the DPDPA to conduct DPIAs and data audits periodically. Other Data Fiduciaries are not statutorily required to, but conducting DPIAs for high-risk launches is strong practice and forms useful evidence of due diligence.

What counts as a "high-risk" processing activity?

Large-scale profiling, processing of children's data, sensitive processing contexts, new AI-driven decisioning, and any activity involving a significant change in purpose or scale are common triggers we screen for.

Can a DPIA be done retroactively for an existing product?

Yes, and we frequently run these as part of a broader Gap Assessment — retroactive DPIAs often surface the highest-value remediation items in the entire programme.
