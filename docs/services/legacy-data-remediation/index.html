Legacy Data Remediation

Bring years of historically collected personal data — with no clear consent trail or purpose record — into a defensible, compliant state.

What it is

Most organisations didn't start collecting personal data the day the DPDPA was enacted. Years of legacy databases, marketing lists, and archived records often have no clear consent trail, unclear or expired purpose, and no retention discipline. We assess this legacy data, classify what can be retained, what requires fresh consent or a Legitimate Uses justification, and what should be securely purged.

Who needs this

Organisations with data collected before a formal privacy programme existed — common after mergers, legacy CRM migrations, or simply years of ungoverned growth.

Our process

  1. Legacy data discovery

    We identify historical databases, archives and lists that predate your current consent and governance practices.

  2. Provenance assessment

    For each dataset, we assess what consent or legal basis evidence exists, if any, and how reliable it is.

  3. Classification & disposition

    Each dataset is classified: retain with existing basis, seek fresh consent, justify under a Legitimate Use, or purge.

  4. Purge or re-consent execution plan

    We design the technical and communication plan for executing purges or re-consent campaigns at scale.

  5. Retention policy going forward

    We close the loop with a retention schedule so legacy data debt doesn't reaccumulate.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need consent for data collected before the DPDPA existed?

The Act generally applies to personal data processed on or after the effective date, regardless of when it was originally collected, and includes provisions addressing data collected before the Act's commencement. In practice this means most legacy holdings need to be brought into a defensible basis rather than assumed grandfathered — we assess this precisely for your specific holdings.

What if we can't reach people to get fresh consent?

Where re-consent isn't practically achievable at scale, purging the unusable portion of the dataset is often the correct and lower-risk disposition — we help you make that call by segment, not as an all-or-nothing decision.

Is this a one-time project?

The initial remediation is a project, but the retention policy we leave you with is designed to prevent the same debt from re-accumulating — that's an ongoing operational discipline, not a one-time fix.
