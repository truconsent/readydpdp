General DPDPA

How is the DPDPA different from GDPR?

The DPDPA shares family resemblance with the EU's GDPR — both centre on consent, notice, and individual rights — but differs in important ways. The DPDPA does not create a separate "special category" of sensitive personal data with distinct rules, as GDPR does. Its cross-border transfer regime is permissive by default, restricted only where the government specifically notifies a restriction, versus GDPR's adequacy-first, restrictive-by-default model. The DPDPA introduces the novel Consent Manager intermediary, which GDPR does not have an equivalent to. Penalty structures, exemption scope, and enforcement mechanics also diverge meaningfully. Organisations that have already built GDPR compliance have a head start conceptually, but a line-by-line mapping exercise is essential — assuming GDPR compliance automatically satisfies the DPDPA is a common and risky mistake.
