Sensitive Personal Data

A nuanced concept: the DPDPA itself does not formally define a separate "sensitive" category, unlike GDPR or India's earlier SPDI Rules.

This is one of the more misunderstood points in DPDPA compliance conversations. Unlike the EU GDPR (with its "special categories") or India's earlier IT Rules SPDI framework (which listed specific sensitive categories like health and financial data), the DPDPA 2023 does not create a separate statutory tier of "sensitive personal data" with distinct handling rules. Every category of personal data is, in principle, subject to the same core obligations. In practice, however, data that would be considered sensitive elsewhere — health records, financial details, biometric data, children's data — still warrants materially higher operational caution, because the harm from mishandling it is greater and because sector regulators (RBI, IRDAI, healthcare bodies) often impose their own heightened handling requirements independently of the DPDPA. We advise clients to keep a practical risk-based sensitivity tiering internally, even though the Act does not formally require one.
